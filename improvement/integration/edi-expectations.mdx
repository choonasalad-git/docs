<Warning>
  EDI is being implemented soon, and there is a empty void of understanding regarding expectations for what EDI can solve. I sense that picking, packing, are being framed as benifiting from EDI but they will not. With integration problems already raised, EDI does one specific thing very well, and that's transport data and translate it.
</Warning>

## The Problem

EDI (Electronic Data Interchange) is a standardised way for **trading partners** to exchange business documents like purchase orders, ship notices, and invoices. It replaces email, PDFs, and portal downloads at the edge of the business.

That is genuinely valuable. But every workflow problem raised in the other pages is an **internal** problem, not a trading-partner document problem. EDI does not touch them.

Also, EDI can only work where it is available so, only a partial of our outbound workflow will even use it as many of our customers do not use EDI. There are definitley questions that need answering before we get to another situation where everuthing is halted because a requirement isn't met.

## What EDI Actually Solves

EDI is the right tool for a specific set of trading-partner problems:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="PO Ingestion" icon="file-input">
    Customer purchase orders arrive as structured EDI documents (typically 850) instead of email or portal downloads.
  </Card>

  <Card title="ASN / Ship Notices" icon="file-output">
    Advance ship notices (856) are transmitted to trading partners automatically once shipments are packed.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Invoicing" icon="file-invoice">
    Invoices (810) go out through EDI, meeting trading-partner compliance requirements.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Inventory Advice" icon="clipboard-list">
    Inventory advice documents (846) can be sent to partners on a schedule.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Fewer Manual Handoffs at the Edge" icon="user-check">
    Staff stop keying customer POs and stop emailing ship confirmations.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Trading-Partner Compliance" icon="shield-check">
    Chargebacks tied to late or missing EDI documents go away when EDI is implemented properly.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Where EDI Does Not Help

Every improvement raised so far lives **inside** the operation, before or after the EDI boundary. Even with EDI running perfectly, these problems remain:

| Problem Area | Still Broken After EDI? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand-based inventory layout congestion | Yes | EDI does not touch physical slotting. |
| Marketplacer CSV import and manual data check | Yes | Marketplacer is not an EDI trading partner in this flow; it is an external app that needs API integration. |
| Generic pick/pack screens | Yes | EDI does not customise screens or workflows. |
| Open future orders driving stock negative | Yes | EDI does not add availability rules or auto-hold logic. |
| Returns handled by spreadsheet and email | Yes | EDI is a forward-flow standard; reverse logistics needs an internal RMA workflow. |
| No pickface replenishment | Yes | EDI has no view of the pickface. |
| Packing bench sorting conflict / paper barcode sheet | Yes | EDI does not create tote identity or a packing screen. |
| Forklift churn from batch-pick reserve pulls | Yes | EDI does not do wave planning. |

<Note>
  Every "Yes" above is a workflow, integration, or customisation problem inside Acumatica and the warehouse. EDI sits at the edge, exchanging documents with outside partners. Different layer, different problem.
</Note>

## Where EDI Actually Helps The Other Improvements

EDI is not useless to these other problems. It helps in one specific way: **cleaner, structured PO ingestion** reduces the amount of bad input hitting the workflow, which makes the internal fixes more visible and more valuable. But EDI is an enabler, not a substitute.

## Recommended Framing for Management

<Steps>
  <Step title="Position EDI accurately">
    EDI is a trading-partner document standard. It solves how POs, ASNs, and invoices move between us and our partners.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Separate internal from external">
    Split the improvement backlog into "trading-partner edge" (EDI territory) and "internal operation" (customisation and integration territory).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sequence realistically">
    EDI go-live does not close the picking, packing, replenishment, integration, or returns items. Those need their own scoped work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set expectations up front">
    Communicate now that EDI is a foundation, not a fix, so the other improvements stay funded and prioritised after go-live.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  If EDI is being sold internally as the answer to everything, the fastest way to correct that is to walk through this table with management before go-live. Every row that remains "Yes" after EDI is a project that still needs to happen.
</Tip>
