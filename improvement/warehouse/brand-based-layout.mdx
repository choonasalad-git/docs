<Warning>
  The current inventory layout is categorised by brand. When two or more orders require items from the same brand, pickers converge on a single aisle and block one another, slowing fulfilment and increasing the risk of picking errors.
</Warning>

## The Problem

Inventory is arranged so that every SKU belonging to a given brand lives together in one section of the warehouse. This looks tidy on paper, but it creates a physical bottleneck during picking.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Aisle Congestion" icon="users">
    Multiple pickers crowd the same brand section whenever concurrent orders share that brand.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Idle Pickers" icon="clock">
    Pickers wait for access to a single bin instead of working in parallel across the warehouse.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Replenishment" icon="truck">
    We are essentially replenishing and picking from one area. This works if the space is large only
  </Card>

  <Card title="Higher Error Rate" icon="alert-triangle">
    Congested aisles and rushed picks lead to mis-picks, wrong quantities, and mixed-up SKUs.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Why brand-based grouping breaks down

<Steps>
  <Step title="Orders arrive in parallel">
    Multiple sales orders enter the fulfilment queue at the same time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Orders share brands">
    A large share of orders contain SKUs from the same top-selling brands.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pickers converge">
    Every picker assigned to those orders is routed to the same physical section.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Throughput drops">
    Only one picker can work efficiently in that section at a time; the rest wait.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## When Can Brand Categorization Work?

Brand can be a useful secondary grouping where: 

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Customers & Space" icon="chart-bar">
    Customer orders are predominantly brand-specific and space allows it.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Pick & Pass" icon="grid-dots">
    Dedicated brand picking or packing teams exist.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Seperation" icon="copy">
    Suppliers retain ownership of consignment stock.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Law" icon="no-copyright">
    Brands require separate security, presentation or compliance.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The main risk is that a brand-only layout places a brand’s fast and slow movers together. Pickers travel farther, prime locations are wasted on slow stock, and operational performance becomes dependent on a commercial classification that may change.

## The Solution

Move away from a purely brand-based layout and toward a **velocity- and dispersion-based slotting strategy**. High-velocity SKUs are spread across the warehouse so that concurrent orders can be picked in parallel without contention.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Velocity Slotting" icon="chart-bar">
    Slot SKUs by pick frequency (A/B/C classes that Acumatica provides) rather than by brand, so fast movers get prime locations.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Zone Picking" icon="grid-dots">
    Divide the warehouse into zones and split popular brands across zones to distribute picker load.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Duplicate Pick Faces" icon="copy">
    Give the highest-velocity SKUs more than one pick location so two pickers can grab the same item at once.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Wave Planning" icon="waves-electricity">
    Release orders in waves that balance brand overlap, keeping pickers spread across the floor.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Recommended implementation

<Steps>
  <Step title="Analyse pick data">
    Pull the last 3 to 6 months of order lines from Acumatica and rank SKUs by pick frequency and by brand overlap across orders.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Classify SKUs">
    Assign each SKU an ABC velocity class. A-class items are the fastest movers and the biggest contributors to congestion.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Redesign the slotting map">
    Distribute A-class SKUs across zones. Keep slow movers grouped where convenient, but never let a single brand monopolise one aisle.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add duplicate pick faces">
    For the top A-class SKUs, add a secondary pick location in a different zone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable zone and wave picking">
    Configure the warehouse management workflow to assign pickers to zones and release orders in balanced waves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Measure and iterate">
    Track picks per hour, aisle wait time, and mis-pick rate. Reslot quarterly as demand patterns shift.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Velocity slotting does not eliminate brand grouping entirely. Slow-moving brands can still be shelved together. The change targets the high-velocity SKUs that cause the congestion.
</Note>

## Expected Outcomes

| Metric | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aisle contention on top brands | High | Low |
| Concurrent pickers per order batch | 1 to 2 effective | 4\+ effective |
| Average pick cycle time | Baseline | 20 to 35% faster |
| Mis-pick rate | Baseline | Reduced |

<Tip>
  Start with the top 20 SKUs by pick frequency. Reslotting that small set typically clears the majority of the congestion before touching the rest of the warehouse.
</Tip>
